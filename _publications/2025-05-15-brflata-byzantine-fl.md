Frequent server–client communication in federated learning (FL) exposes model parameters to interception and tampering. We propose BRFLATA (Byzantine-robust FL by adaptive tripartite authentication), which comprises four modules: an adaptive client matching mechanism based on a credibility table, privacy-preserving client authentication, a reliable communication link secured by RSA signatures and zero-knowledge proofs, and credibility-weighted global aggregation driven by an AIMD incentive mechanism. BRFLATA authenticates every client, detects Byzantine clients and link attackers, and limits their influence on the global model. Experiments on AG-News, Fashion-MNIST, and UK-DALE show consistent robustness across attack types, with the largest gains when Byzantine clients make up a high proportion of participants.
